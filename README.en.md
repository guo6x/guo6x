## My north star

**Help make space exploration accessible to more ordinary people.**

This is not something I can accomplish with one app or a few lines of code. Aerospace demands manufacturing, controls, materials, testing, capital, teams and uncompromising attention to safety and evidence.

My approach is to start where I can contribute now: **solve real problems with AI and software, develop commercial and engineering capabilities, and gradually apply those capabilities toward aerospace.** The exact route should evolve with evidence rather than being fixed prematurely.

> **Build. Test. Learn. Repeat.**  
> Make it real, verify the result, fix what fails, keep going.

## About me

I'm **Jiaji Guo (郭家吉)**, an undergraduate student in **Aircraft Manufacturing Engineering** at the **Science and Technology College of Nanchang Hangkong University**, enrolled in 2024.

Alongside my studies, I build AI software, open-source tools and aerospace engineering experiments. In summer 2026, I completed an internship in Hangzhou working on AI workflows and collaboration software.

I enjoy crossing disciplines, but I do not confuse a working prototype with a delivered product—or a passing software test with physical-flight verification.

### Problems I'm working on

- **AI agents & engineering software:** How do agents assess evidence, use tools, validate their outcomes and revise mistaken decisions?
- **Aerospace & control engineering:** How can dynamics, controls, simulation and embedded systems become testable engineering systems?
- **Products & entrepreneurship:** Can an application demonstrably save users time, reduce costs or prevent errors?
- **Long-term exploration:** How can technology, organizations and resources be built toward broader access to space?

## A practical path toward space

1. **Now:** Find genuine user problems, develop AI tools and test whether they deliver useful value.
2. **Next:** Build deeper skills in mechanical engineering, controls, testing and integrated hardware-software systems.
3. **Long term:** Work toward the capabilities, resources and teams needed to help build aerospace ventures and broaden participation in space exploration.

These are directions for exploration—not a promised timeline or a claim about a funded venture.
