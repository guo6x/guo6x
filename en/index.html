The long-term mission

Not a slogan. A goal that will take years of work.

I do not want to stop at being a developer who writes code, or at launching a single AI product. My ultimate direction is to make participation in space exploration possible for more people.

That ambition requires capital, teams, manufacturing, controls, safety, fundamental science and sustained engineering progress. I cannot deliver it today. My practical approach is to begin where I can contribute: build AI products that solve real problems, learn from commercial and user feedback, and steadily develop engineering capability that can be independently tested. The vision is ambitious; the evidence must remain honest.

01

Solve real problems with AI

Start with genuine needs and real users; build tools that save time, reduce costs or prevent avoidable errors.

02

Earn engineering credibility

Connect software, controls, simulation, mechanical design and testing without confusing prototypes with qualification.

03

Work toward accessible space

Build skills, resources and relationships over time; keep the route flexible as evidence and opportunities evolve.

Background & motivation

Who I am.
Why I build.

I began my undergraduate studies in Aircraft Manufacturing Engineering in 2024. Alongside university, I build open-source software and hands-on experiments to bridge the gap between coursework and real-world engineering.

What excites me is not a stack of technical buzzwords, but whether technology can change something for the better. The next chapter may involve a startup, an engineering team or further study; the route remains open while the north star stays the same.

Outside engineering, I hope to make my own music, improve at piano and spend time traveling and exploring nature. I want a life that is curious in more than one direction.
